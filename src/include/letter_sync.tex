\section {A letter to a hundred people (Probability)}

You get a letter that says: ``This letter was sent to exactly 100 people and you are one of them.
You may reply to the return address or not. If I get exactly one reply, each of the 100 people will get a million dollars.
If I get no reply, or more than one reply, nobody gets anything.''

You cannot communicate with the other 99 people, and they are all at least as smart as you are.
What should you do to maximize the chance of winning?

\subsection{Solution}

There is no way to break the symmetry between the 100 people: everybody is in exactly the same situation and is as smart as you,
so whatever you decide, they decide too. If you decide to reply then everybody replies, and if you decide not to reply then nobody does.
Either way nobody wins.

The way out is to use randomness: reply with some probability $p$, for example by rolling dice.
Everybody else will compute the same $p$. The letter writer gets exactly one reply if one person replies and the other 99 do not.
There are 100 ways to choose that person, so the chance of winning is
\begin{center}
$f(p) = 100p(1-p)^{99}$
\end{center}
To find the maximum, take the derivative:
\begin{center}
$f'(p) = 100(1-p)^{99} - 100 \cdot 99 p (1-p)^{98} = 100(1-p)^{98}(1-p-99p)$
\end{center}
It is zero when $1 - 100p = 0$, that is, at $p = \frac{1}{100}$.
So intuition is right: each person should reply with probability $\frac{1}{100}$, so that on average exactly one person replies.

The chance of winning is then $\left(1-\frac{1}{100}\right)^{99} \approx 0.370$, which is surprisingly high.
With $n$ people the chance is $\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n-1}$, which tends to $\frac{1}{e} \approx 0.368$ as $n$ grows.
So even with a million people the chance of winning is still almost $37\%$.
