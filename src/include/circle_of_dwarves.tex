\section {Circle of dwarves (Probability)}

$N$ dwarves stand in a circle and a black or a white hat is placed on the head of each, at random.
No dwarf can see his own hat but every dwarf sees the hats of all the others.
No communication is possible once the hats are on. At some moment all the dwarves must shout, at the same time, the color of their own hat.
They want to maximize the chance that \emph{all} of them are right. What strategy should they plan beforehand?
How good can it be?

\subsection{Solution}

They agree to assume that the total number of black hats is even.
Each dwarf counts the black hats he sees. If the count is even he shouts ``white'' and if it is odd he shouts ``black'',
which is exactly the color that makes the total even.

If the total number of black hats is indeed even then all the dwarves are right, and if it is odd then all of them are wrong.
The total is even with probability $1/2$, so they all survive with probability $1/2$.

This cannot be improved: the first dwarf alone is right with probability $1/2$, since he knows nothing about his own hat,
so all of them together cannot be right more often than that.

It seems strange that $N$ dwarves, each of whom guesses right only half of the time, are all right half of the time.
The trick is that the strategy does not make anyone guess better. It makes the mistakes happen together:
whenever one of the dwarves is wrong, all of them are wrong.
