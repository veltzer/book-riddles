\section {Birthing girls (Probability)}

A village elder wants more women in the village. He tells the villagers:
every family should keep having babies until it has a daughter, and then stop.
Will this make the village have more girls than boys?

\subsection{Solution}

No. The ratio of boys to girls will stay $1:1$.

The short explanation: every birth is a boy or a girl with probability $1/2$, independently of all previous births.
The rule only decides when a family stops, it cannot change the outcome of any single birth.
So on average half of all the babies born are girls, no matter what the rule for stopping is.

Here is another way to see it. Let the villagers have as many babies as they like, until every family has a daughter.
Here the ratio is clearly $1:1$. Now remove every child that has an older sister.
Each removed child is a boy or a girl with probability $1/2$, so the removed children are also $1:1$, and so are the
children that are left. But the children that are left are exactly what the elder's rule produces.

And here is the calculation. Every family has exactly one girl.
A family has $k$ boys if its first $k$ babies are boys and the next one is a girl, which happens with probability $(1/2)^{k+1}$.
So the expected number of boys in a family is
\begin{center}
$s = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{2^{k+1}} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{8} + \frac{3}{16} + \ldots$
\end{center}
Subtracting $\frac{s}{2} = \frac{1}{8} + \frac{2}{16} + \ldots$ gives
\begin{center}
$\frac{s}{2} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{16} + \ldots = \frac{1}{2}$
\end{center}
so $s=1$: on average every family has one boy and one girl.

\subsection{Follow up}
What if the elder had said ``stop when you have six girls for every boy''?
Unlike the previous rule, this condition may never happen. What are the chances that a family will ever stop?

\subsection{Solution to the follow up}

About $5.7\%$. Almost every family goes on forever.

Keep track of $x = \textrm{girls} - 6\cdot\textrm{boys}$. It starts at $0$, every girl adds $1$ and every boy subtracts $6$,
and the family stops when $x$ is back at $0$. On average a birth changes $x$ by $\frac{1-6}{2} = -2.5$,
so $x$ drifts away towards $-\infty$ and a return to $0$ is far from certain.

The family can only be back at $0$ after $7m$ births, $m$ of them boys, which happens with probability $\binom{7m}{m}/2^{7m}$.
So the expected number of times $x$ is at $0$, counting the start, is
\begin{center}
$V = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{\binom{7m}{m}}{2^{7m}} = 1 + \frac{7}{128} + \frac{91}{16384} + \ldots \approx 1.06096$
\end{center}
Now let $p$ be the chance of ever coming back to $0$. Every time $x$ is at $0$ the future looks the same as at the start,
so $x$ visits $0$ exactly $k$ times with probability $p^{k-1}(1-p)$, and the expected number of visits is $V = \frac{1}{1-p}$.
Therefore
\begin{center}
$p = 1 - \frac{1}{V} \approx 0.05746$
\end{center}
Most of it is the very first chance: $6$ girls and $1$ boy in the first $7$ births has probability $\frac{7}{128} \approx 5.47\%$,
and all the later chances together add only about $0.28\%$.
(The sum has a closed form: $p = \frac{7(2r-1)}{2r}$, where $r \approx 0.50414$ is the root of $r^7 - 2r + 1 = 0$ between $\frac{1}{2}$ and $1$.)
