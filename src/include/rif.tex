\section {Reduction in force (Logic)}

A boss tells his 100 employees that tomorrow there will be a reduction in force.
He will stand them in a line, one behind the other, all facing the same direction, so that each of them sees everyone in front of them but nobody behind them.
He will put a white or a black cap on the head of each of them. Then each one in turn will say out loud the color of their own cap.
Whoever is right keeps their job and whoever is wrong is fired.
The employees may decide in advance in which order they speak, and agree on a strategy.
How many of them can be sure to keep their job?

\subsection{Solution}

$99$ of them can be sure to keep their job, and the last one has a $50\%$ chance.

Think of white as $0$ and black as $1$. The employees speak from the back of the line to the front.
The one at the back sees all the other $99$ caps. She adds them up and says the color that makes the sum of all $100$ numbers even:
``white'' if the sum she sees is even and ``black'' if it is odd. Her own cap has nothing to do with it, so she is right with probability $1/2$.

Now everyone knows that the sum of the $99$ caps in front of her is even if she said ``white'' and odd if she said ``black''.
Each of the following employees knows the color of every one of those $99$ caps except their own: they see the caps in front of them,
and every employee who spoke before them, except the one at the back, said their color correctly.
So they can compute their own color as the one that gives the known parity, and they are all right.
