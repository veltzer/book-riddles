\section {50 dollars spent (Arithmetic)}

I have 50 dollars and I spend them in four purchases.
After each purchase I write down how much I spent and how much I have left:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r r}
I spend & Left \\
\hline
20 & 30 \\
15 & 15 \\
9 & 6 \\
6 & 0 \\
\hline
50 & 51 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The first column sums to 50, as it should, but the second column sums to 51.
Where did the extra dollar come from?

\subsection{Solution}

There is no extra dollar. The sum of the second column has no reason to be equal to 50 and the fact that it is close to 50 is a coincidence.

The second column is a list of balances, and adding up balances at different points in time does not measure anything.
If I spent the whole 50 dollars at once the second column would sum to $0$.
If I spent the 50 dollars one dollar at a time the second column would sum to $49+48+\cdots+1+0 = \frac{49 \cdot 50}{2} = 1225$.
Only the first column has to sum to 50.
