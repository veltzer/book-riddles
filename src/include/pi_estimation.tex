\section {Estimating $\pi$ (Algorithms)}

Write a program that gives better and better estimates of $\pi$ without using a series like the Taylor series.
The program should be easy to write and deterministic: the longer it runs, the better the estimate is guaranteed to be.

\subsection{Solution}

A popular answer is to pick random points in a square of side $1$ and count how many of them fall inside the circle of diameter $1$ drawn in it.
The circle has area $\pi/4$ and the square has area $1$, so $4$ times the fraction of points inside the circle estimates $\pi$.
But this does not meet the requirements: it depends on a good random number generator,
and more samples only make a good estimate likely, they do not guarantee it.

Instead, count squares deterministically. Cover the quarter of the circle of radius $N$ that lies in the first quadrant with a grid of $N \times N$ unit squares.
The quarter circle has area $\frac{\pi N^2}{4}$.
Let $L$ be the number of squares that lie completely inside the circle: the square whose far corner is $(i,j)$ is inside when $i^2+j^2 \le N^2$.
Let $U$ be the number of squares that touch the inside of the circle: the square whose near corner is $(i,j)$ touches it when $i^2+j^2 < N^2$.
Then
\begin{center}
$\frac{4L}{N^2} \le \pi \le \frac{4U}{N^2}$
\end{center}
The squares counted in $U$ but not in $L$ are the ones that the arc of the circle crosses. Going along the arc, every such square starts one step to the
right or one step down from the previous one, so there are fewer than $2N$ of them and the two bounds are less than $\frac{8}{N}$ apart.

Counting the squares is easy: in column $i$ there are $\lfloor \sqrt{N^2-i^2} \rfloor$ squares completely inside the circle.
Run the program with $N = 10, 100, 1000, \ldots$ and you get guaranteed bounds that improve with every step:
$N=1000$ gives $3.1375 \le \pi \le 3.1456$ and $N=10000$ gives $3.1411 \le \pi \le 3.1420$.
