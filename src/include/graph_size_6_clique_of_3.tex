\section {Six people (Graph theory)}

Prove that in every group of 6 people there are either three people who all know each other or three people none of whom knows the others.

In the language of graphs: color every edge of the complete graph on 6 vertices red or blue.
Prove that there is a triangle whose edges all have the same color.

\subsection{Solution}

Pick any person $A$. There are 5 other people, so by the pigeonhole principle $A$ either knows at least 3 of them or does not know at least 3 of them.

Suppose $A$ knows three people $B$, $C$ and $D$. If any two of $B$, $C$, $D$ know each other then they form, together with $A$,
three people who all know each other. Otherwise $B$, $C$ and $D$ are three people none of whom knows the others.

If $A$ does not know three people $B$, $C$ and $D$ the argument is the same with ``know'' and ``don't know'' swapped.

The number 6 cannot be lowered: seat 5 people around a table and let everybody know exactly their two neighbours.
There are no three people who all know each other and no three people none of whom knows the others.
