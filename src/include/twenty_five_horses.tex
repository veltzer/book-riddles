\section{25 horses, 3 best (Algorithms)}
You need to find the 3 fastest horses out of 25. You can only race 5 at a time and you don't have a timer. What's
the minimum number of races you need and who will you race?

\subsection{Solution}
Let's mark all horses as $H_1$ - $H_{25}$.
First race 5 races $H_1$-$H_5$, $H_6$-$H_{10}$, $H_{11}$-$H_{15}$, $H_{16}$-$H_{20}$, $H_{21}$-$H_{25}$.
Denote the winners as $W_{1,5}$, $W_{6,10}$, $W_{11,15}$, $W_{16,20}$, $W_{21,25}$. 
Second runners up will be $SRU_{1,5}$, $SRU_{6,10}$, $SRU_{11,15}$, $SRU_{16,20}$, $SRU_{21,25}$.
Third runners up will be $TRU_{1,5}$, $TRU_{6,10}$, $TRU_{11,15}$, $TRU_{16,20}$, $TRU_{21,25}$.
We will race all the winners and get $WW_1$ - $WW_5$.

Now let's use some logic.

$WW_1$ is the fastest around so we don't need to race him again and he will be the top in the top 3.

Discard anyone in the group that got won by $WW_4$ and $WW_5$ since they got beat by at least three horses ($WW_1, WW_2, WW_3$) so they, and by inference anyone they beat, cannot be in the top 3.

Discard anyone in the group that got won by $WW_3$ but not $WW_3$ himself. This is because there are at least two horses better than $WW_3$ so anyone that $WW_3$ beat cannot be in the top 3 but $WW_3$ maybe the third in the entire group. So $WW_3$ joins the final race.

In the group of horses won by $WW_2$ we can discard everyone but $WW_2$ and the second runner up. Third runner up and down have at least 3 horses better than them ($SRU_{WW_2}$, $WW_2$ and $WW_1$). So $WW_2$ and $SRU_{WW_2}$ joins the race.

In the group of $WW_1$ we can discard everyone but the second and third runners up. So $SRU_{WW_1}$ and $TRU_{WW_1}$ join the race.

So now we will race $WW_3$, $WW_2$, $SRU_{WW_2}$, $SRU_{WW_1}$, $TRU_{WW_1}$. The first two will join $WW_1$ in being the fastest 3.

Number of races: 7

Why can't it be done in $6$? Imagine that the results are decided by a spiteful demon, who only has to stay
consistent with some real order of the horses. Call a horse \emph{unbeaten} as long as it has not finished
behind any other horse. An unbeaten horse could still be the fastest, so when we are done exactly one horse
may be unbeaten. The demon's rule is that in every race the unbeaten horses finish ahead of the rest. This
never contradicts anything already known, since no horse is known to be faster than an unbeaten one. So a
race with $k$ unbeaten horses beats at most $k-1\le4$ of them for the first time, and we need $24$ first
defeats, which takes at least $6$ races. And with only $6$ races, every race must be made of $5$ unbeaten
horses, so a horse that has been beaten never races again.

Now look at the 6th race. The first $5$ races beat $20$ different horses, and the winner of the 5th race is
not one of them, so at least $21$ horses raced before the 6th race and at most $4$ did not. So some horse in
the 6th race has raced before, and since it is unbeaten it won every race it ran. The demon lets it win the
6th race too; call it $C$. Let $Q$ be the runner-up of an earlier race won by $C$, and $R$ and $T$ the second
and third horses of the 6th race. $Q$ finished behind $C$ only and never raced again, and $R$ and $T$ never
lost before the 6th race, so nothing links $Q$ to $R$ or $T$. Both orders $C,Q,R,T$ and $C,R,T,Q$ (with all
the other horses behind) agree with every result, and they have different top $3$s. So $6$ races are never
enough, and $7$ is the minimum.
