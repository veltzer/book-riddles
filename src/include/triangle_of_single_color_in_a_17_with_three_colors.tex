\section {Seventeen points and three colors (Combinatorics)}
% follows: graph_size_6_clique_of_3

Every edge of the complete graph on 17 vertices is colored with one of three colors.
Prove that there is a triangle whose edges all have the same color.

\subsection{Solution}

This builds on the previous riddle, which shows that if the edges of the complete graph on 6 vertices are colored with
two colors then there is a triangle of a single color.

Pick any vertex $A$. It has 16 edges, each in one of three colors, so by the pigeonhole principle at least $6$ of them
have the same color, say red. Let $B_1, \ldots, B_6$ be the other ends of these red edges.

If any edge $B_iB_j$ is red then $A$, $B_i$, $B_j$ is a red triangle.
Otherwise all the edges between $B_1, \ldots, B_6$ are colored with only the two other colors.
That is a complete graph on 6 vertices colored with two colors, so it has a triangle of a single color.
