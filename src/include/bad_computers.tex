\section {Finding a good computer (Algorithms)}

There are 100 computers. At least 51 of them are good and the rest, at most 49, are broken.
You may ask computer $A$ whether computer $B$ is good or broken.
If $A$ is good its answer is correct, but if $A$ is broken its answer can be anything.
Find a good computer while asking as few questions as possible in the worst case.

\subsection{Solution}

First set one computer aside and never use it. Among the remaining 99 computers at least 50 are good and at most 49 are broken,
so the good computers are still a strict majority.

We build a chain of computers in which every computer has said that the next one is good.
Go over the 99 computers one by one. For each computer $x$:
if the chain is empty, put $x$ on the chain without asking anything.
Otherwise ask the computer at the top of the chain about $x$.
If it says that $x$ is good, put $x$ at the top of the chain.
If it says that $x$ is broken, remove the top of the chain and throw away both it and $x$.
When all the computers are processed, the computer at the top of the chain is good.

Why does this work?
\begin{enumerate}
\item If a good computer is on the chain then the computer after it on the chain is good too, since a good computer vouched for it.
So the chain is made of some broken computers followed by some good computers, and if there is any good computer on the chain then the top is good.
\item Whenever we throw away two computers, at least one of them is broken: if the top of the chain is good then its answer is correct and $x$ is broken,
and if it is not good then it is broken itself.
So throwing away a pair never hurts the majority: among the computers that were not thrown away the good ones remain a strict majority.
\item At the end all the computers that were not thrown away are on the chain. They have a good majority so the chain is not empty and holds a good computer,
which means that its top is good.
\end{enumerate}

Every computer except the first costs at most one question, so this protocol asks at most 98 questions.
