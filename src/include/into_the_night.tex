\section {Into the night (Arithmetic)}

This riddle was invented after watching the Netflix series ``Into the Night''.

Assume that the circumference of the earth along the equator is 48,000 km, that a plane flies at 1,000 km/h,
and that night is exactly half of the day. You fly along the equator and want to stay in the dark for as long as possible.
Should you fly east or west, and for how long and how far can you fly?

\subsection{Solution}

Night covers half of the equator, a band of 24,000 km.
The earth turns once a day, so this band moves west along the equator at $48000/24 = 2000$ km/h.
Its western edge is dusk and its eastern edge is dawn.
In both directions it is best to take off at dusk, when the whole band of night is still ahead of the sun.

\textbf{Flying east.} Dawn is 24,000 km east of you and moves toward you at 2,000 km/h while you fly toward it at 1,000 km/h.
If you fly for $n$ hours then $1000n + 2000n = 24000$, so $n=8$ and you cover 8,000 km.

\textbf{Flying west.} Dusk runs away from you, since it moves west faster than you do.
Dawn is 24,000 km behind you and catches up at $2000 - 1000 = 1000$ km/h.
So $2000n = 1000n + 24000$, which gives $n=24$ and you cover 24,000 km.

So you should fly west: you can stay in the dark for a whole day and cover half of the globe.
Going around the globe in the dark takes two such flights when flying west, but six when flying east.
